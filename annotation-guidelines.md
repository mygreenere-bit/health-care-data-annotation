 Healthcare Text Annotation Guidelines

1. Purpose

These guidelines establish consistent rules for classifying fictional healthcare statements into predefined categories.

 2. Annotation Categories

CONDITION
Use this label when the statement describes a diagnosed disease, injury, or medical condition.

Example: The patient has a fracture of the right femur.

SYMPTOM
Use this label when the statement describes a symptom reported by a patient or an observed clinical sign.

Example: The patient reports severe knee pain.

TREATMENT
Use this label when the statement describes a medical or rehabilitation intervention.

Example: The patient underwent physiotherapy.

FUNCTION
Use this label when the statement describes a patient's ability or limitation in performing activities.

Example: The patient cannot walk independently.

DEVICE
Use this label when the statement identifies a medical or assistive device.

Example: The patient uses a below-knee prosthetic limb.

 3. Annotation Rules

1. Read the complete statement before assigning a label.
2. Identify the primary information being classified.
3. Select the most appropriate category.
4. Do not infer a diagnosis that is not stated.
5. Apply the same definitions consistently.
6. Flag ambiguous statements for review rather than guessing.

 4. Quality Control

Each annotation should be reviewed for:
- Correct category selection.
- Consistency with the guidelines.
- Accurate interpretation of the statement.
- Completeness of the annotation record.

 5. Scope

These guidelines are designed for educational practice in healthcare text annotation. They are not intended for clinical diagnosis or treatment decisions.
